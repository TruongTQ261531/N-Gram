# Related-work update after primary confirmation

Checked 29 October 2026, before the independent replication analysis. This is a targeted audit, not an exhaustive Scopus/Web of Science review or an originality certificate. Query strings, returned titles/URLs, failures and source-access levels are saved under `audit/`. The initial search used some overly long queries that returned no results; shorter/title searches found additional relevant papers. A zero-result search is not evidence of novelty. ## Current directions and their implications

| Source | What was inspected | Overlap and distinction |
|---|---|---|
| [RobustMask, 2512.23307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23307) | Full method Sections 3–4 | Certified top-K neural reranking under document manipulation and randomized masking is prior art. It changes the smoothed model and perturbation domain. Its percentages cannot be used as matched baselines for our unchanged character-BM25 and common query edits. |
| [Certified Error Control of Candidate Set Pruning, 2205.09638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09638) | Full text calibration/guarantee construction | Certified retrieval pruning is already established. It controls population risk using calibration/exchangeability; our exclusions are deterministic within one finite query lattice. We do not claim inventing certified pruning. |
| [CertEx, 2610.21325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21325) | Full text exclusion/rounding mechanism | Conservative low-precision exclusion and floating-point retrieval certificates are current prior art. It guarantees exact geometric kNN for a fixed query, not worst rank under a shared discrete text-edit lattice. Numerical guards and whole-corpus screening are not general firsts here. |
| [Certified Sparse Attention via Perturbed Retrieval, 2610.24991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24991) | Abstract | Query-level certificates for attention approximation, randomized sampling and stopping. Different target and perturbation semantics; abstract-only screening does not establish detailed separation from every component. |
| [CertBind, 2608.06516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06516) | Full text related work and certificate assumptions | Shared failures, retrieval decision certificates, top-K covered sets and abstention already appear in multimodal composition. It uses anchor/connector geometry and conformal calibration. Do not claim that decision-level retrieval certification itself is new. |
| [Certified Domain Consistency / C3R, 2607.14157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14157) | Full text label constraint and guarantee discussion | Label-free query-time control and abstention are current directions. Its target is domain contamination with calibrated risk, not finite character edits. “No new labels” alone is not novelty. |
| [RAGSentinel, 2608.23965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23965) | Abstract | A training-free, label-free geometric defense under majority/separation assumptions. Different document-poisoning target; these general deployment properties are not new. |
| [RobustRAG, 2405.15556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15556), [C-RAG, 2402.03181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03181) | Abstracts | Retrieval corruption certificates and conformal generation-risk control already exist. We have not run downstream generation, so make no RAG-answer quality claim. |
| [Understanding and Debugging Failures in N-Gram-Based Generative Retrieval, 2606.17721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17721) | Full text empirical/diagnostic sections | N-gram identifier ambiguity, low diversity, lexical mismatch and additive evidence are active topics. Those generative SEAL/MINDER systems are different from character-BM25. Adding ngrams or an audit dashboard alone would not supply the desired method contribution. |
| [PersonalQuery-Benchmark, 2610.32164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32164) | Abstract | Personalized expression and writing-error robustness are already evaluated across retrieval architectures with LLM/human validation. Our project avoids creating another annotation-heavy benchmark and does not claim semantic equivalence of arbitrary edits. |
| [CostCert, 2507.23335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23335) | Full text Sections 3.1–3.2 and tie-cost mechanism | **Substantial conceptual overlap:** independent pairwise top-K comparisons inflate a shared attacker budget; a joint count/cost argument can tighten certification without exhaustive allocation. This motivation is not new. CostCert exploits integer voting structure and clean votes under image patch attacks. Our finite text states induce arbitrary real additive document margins, with several competitors affected by one token choice; its vote-allocation algorithm cannot simply be applied unchanged. This is a required close comparison in a submission. |
| [Certified Adaptive Selection, 2610.14996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14996) | Abstract | Exact top-K selection from partial outcome evaluations and guaranteed score intervals already exists. Different object: recovering a fixed decision with acquisition costs, rather than bounding adversarial rank over common text edits. |
| [CertMoE, 2610.29395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29395) | Abstract | Relaxed top-K containment and a larger certified pool are current verification tools. A containment target alone is not our novelty. |
| [Almost Tight L0 Top-K Certification, 2011.07633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07633), [Top-K Randomized Smoothing, 1912.09899](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09899) | Abstracts | Top-K robustness under a common perturbed input and bounded changed features substantially predates this project. These smooth a general classifier; our object is the unchanged additive scorer and finite enumerated token states. Neither top-K nor a changed-token budget supplies novelty by itself. |

These recent arXiv sources are treated as preprints unless their publication status is separately verified. Reading an abstract is explicitly weaker than reviewing a complete method. Original papers linked in [NOVELTY.md](NOVELTY.md), especially CertDR, relaxed local robustness and convex verification, remain essential prior art.

## Defensible contribution hypothesis

The potentially publishable unit is a **specific certifier for finite common-query edits of a fixed additive ngram ranker**: exact pairwise screening, a matched shared-query LP, and conservative discrete incompatibility covers whose utility survives feasible enumeration. Its value must come from a stronger or cheaper certificate with visible proof traces, not a new name for LP, DP, graph coloring, BM25, pruning or top-K stability.

The generic additive-factor construction proves that discrete covers can beat this particular convex relaxation by an unbounded factor. It does not establish that this theorem is unprecedented in verification/optimization literature, nor that every instance is realizable by the fixed keyboard/BM25 setting. The real-data improvement and isolated runtime costs must therefore be reported alongside the theorem and the generic MILP comparator.

## Remaining novelty risks

Conflict graphs, mutually exclusive violations, disjunctive optimization and combinatorial robustness have a long literature outside IR. This audit does not rule out an equivalent reduction there. A submission should undergo a dedicated closest-method review and an independent mathematical/code review. No claim of Q1 readiness or journal acceptance follows from search coverage, significance, test counts or CI. The publisher scope/quartile was not independently verified from current primary JCR/SJR records in this run; no journal ranking is asserted.
